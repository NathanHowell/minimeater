# 5 V and 3.3 V rail transients in ngspice

Generated by `uv run minimeater-sim` (`src/minimeater/sim/rails.py`). The whole power path of the schematic: USB source (V, cable R and L, or a current-limited port) - D1 and C38/C41 - U12 eFuse (as in `power.md`, with its 3.65 V falling UVLO) - VBUS_F (C1, C2, C4, 100 nF, derated) - U4 (3V3), U5 behind FB1 (3V3A), the U6 servo and U7 fan switches into their output capacitors and loads. The module's draw is 80 mA idle and 352 mA in a TX burst (ADR 0004), edges 1 us.

No vendor model of the TLV62569 runs in ngspice, so U4 is two behavioural halves. Its **input** is a constant-power load (93 % efficient, UVLO 2.45 V on / 2.3 V off, 0.8 ms soft start): that is what the 5 V rail sees. Its **output** is the regulated 3V3 less the load step's droop from the datasheet's own transient capture (SLVSDG1C Figure 18: a 1.2 A step at 1 A/us dips 0.2 V and recovers in about 4 us, so 0.167 V per amp and 4 us, as a high-pass on the load current), less dropout when VBUS_F is too low to regulate. A PI loop tuned to that capture did not behave like the part (DCS-Control has far more DC gain than the capture can set), so the capture is used as measured. The 3V3 set point is the low end of its window for the minima (VFB 0.588 V, 1 % resistors: 3.199 V) and the high end for the overshoots (3.440 V). The module needs 3.0 V (ESP32-C6-WROOM-1 VDD33 minimum `[spec]`); the brownout detector's threshold is set in firmware (not read here).

## 3V3 budget

| | |
|---|---|
| Regulation window, low end | 3.199 V |
| TX burst step (80 to 352 mA) | -45 mV |
| PSM ripple, scaled from Figure 15 (0.1 V pk-pk on about 7.5 uF, same charge per pulse, 17.0 uF here) `[est]` | -44 mV |
| **Lowest 3V3** | **3.109 V** |
| Margin to the module's 3.0 V | 109 mV |

U4 regulates until VBUS_F falls to 3.32 V + 0.35 A x 165 mohm = 3.38 V; the eFuse's UVLO turns the board off at 3.65 V, so the first thing to give on a sagging 5 V is the whole board, not the 3V3 rail. 3V3A stays at 3.30 V in every case below (a 3.3 V LDO from at least 4.4 V).

## Plug-in and power-on

| Source | 3V3 reaches 3.0 V | 3V3 peak | VBUS_F peak | VBUS_RAW peak | Source current peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.25 V, 0.05 ohm, 2 uH cable | 1.68 ms | 3.44 V | 5.28 V | 7.11 V | 1.38 A |
| 5 V, 0.1 ohm, 1 uH cable | 1.68 ms | 3.44 V | 5.00 V | 7.13 V | 1.60 A |
| 4.75 V, 0.3 ohm, 0.25 uH cable | 1.68 ms | 3.44 V | 4.75 V | 5.78 V | 1.42 A |
| 0.5 A source (4.75 V, 0.3 ohm), loads gated | 1.68 ms | 3.44 V | 4.75 V | 4.76 V | 0.50 A |

VBUS_F follows the eFuse's 4.2 V/ms ramp after its 0.4 ms delay, the buck starts at 2.45 V and ramps 3V3 over 0.8 ms: 3V3 is up 1.7 ms after the plug-in, with no overshoot beyond the set point's window. The cable rings VBUS_RAW to about 7 V as in `power.md`; VBUS_F never passes the source voltage. A 0.5 A port boots the board with the loads gated: the derated bulk takes C dV/dt = 0.26 A (0.5 A with nominal capacitors) and the module's 80 mA.

## TX bursts

Three 0.5 ms bursts, 1 ms apart. Set point at the low end of its window.

| Source | 3V3 minimum | 3V3 maximum | VBUS_F minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.0 V, 0.1 ohm | 3.159 V | 3.239 V | 4.96 V |
| 4.75 V, 0.3 ohm, 1.5 A source | 3.159 V | 3.239 V | 4.72 V |
| 4.75 V, 0.3 ohm, 0.5 A source (loads gated) | 3.159 V | 3.239 V | 4.72 V |

The burst takes 0.25 A from VBUS_F; the 5 V side drops by the source's resistance (about 75 mV at 0.3 ohm) and the 3V3 droop is the datasheet's, 45 mV. The 3V3 rises by the same amount when the burst ends.

## Fan, servo stall and a TX burst together

The fan is switched on at 7 ms, the servo at 8 ms with its rotor locked (the switch holds it at its 0.6 A limit, `dissipation.md`), then three TX bursts from 9 ms. Set point low.

| Source | 3V3 minimum | 3V3A minimum | VBUS_F minimum | VBUS_RAW minimum | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.0 V, 0.1 ohm, 1 uH | 3.159 V | 3.30 V | 4.86 V | 4.89 V | holds |
| 4.75 V, 0.3 ohm, 2 uH | 3.159 V | 3.30 V | 4.40 V | 4.44 V | holds |
| 4.75 V, 0.3 ohm, 1.5 A source | 3.159 V | 3.30 V | 4.62 V | 4.65 V | holds |
| 4.75 V, 0.3 ohm, 1.0 A source | 3.159 V | 3.30 V | 4.25 V | 4.28 V | holds |
| 4.75 V, 0.3 ohm, 0.5 A source | 3.187 V | 3.30 V | 3.65 V | 3.67 V | **brownout** 0.22 ms after the servo switch-on: VBUS_RAW falls to the eFuse's UVLO |

On a 1.5 A source at its worst (4.75 V, 0.3 ohm of cable) the rail never goes under 4.6 V and 3V3 stays above 3.15 V (3.11 V with the PSM ripple of the budget above). On a 0.5 A port the servo's switch-on alone takes the 5 V down to the eFuse's UVLO in a fifth of a millisecond (the run ends there): the board would reset, and again each time the servo were enabled after the reboot. That is ADR 0004's amendment in a waveform: the supply check must keep the servo off until CC says 1.5 A, because no capacitor can bridge it (the servo's 0.6 A limit plus the fan and radio is 1.1 A against 0.5 A). A 1.0 A port holds in this test, but only because the bursts are short and the 5 V sags to 4.3 V; a real port that folds back at its limit may not. The model's source is ideal up to its limit (no regulation delay, no foldback or hiccup).

## Bulk capacitance

The worst chain on a 4.75 V, 0.3 ohm, 2 uH cable, with the VBUS_F bulk (C1, 100 uF 1210, 45 uF derated) swept:

| C1 | VBUS_F minimum | 3V3 minimum |
|---|---|---|
| C1 omitted | 4.39 V | 3.159 V |
| C1 = 10 uF (derated) | 4.40 V | 3.159 V |
| C1 = 22 uF (derated) | 4.40 V | 3.159 V |
| C1 = 45 uF (derated) | 4.40 V | 3.159 V |
| C1 = 90 uF (derated) | 4.40 V | 3.159 V |

The rail's margin does not come from the bulk. Taking C1 out changes VBUS_F's minimum by 10-20 mV: the minimum is the cable's drop (0.3 ohm x 0.85 A), and C2, C4 and the 100 nF caps already carry the sub-microsecond steps. The bulk also does not ride through a source interruption: the TPS259531's feature list has no reverse-current blocking, so its output discharges back into the cable. What it costs is the plug-in current, C dV/dt at the eFuse's 4.2 V/ms (0.26-0.5 A for the 100 uF). So 22 uF would hold every scenario here; C1 stays at 100 uF because it is already on the BOM (the part C36 uses too), ADR 0004 sized it (100-220 uF) to absorb the servo's start and the WiFi bursts, which a real charger's slower regulation needs more than this model's ideal source does, and it does not endanger the 0.5 A port's boot. If bring-up shows ripple on VBUS_F at the servo's PWM or the radio, the capacitance is the first thing to look at; if it does not, there is room to drop it.

## Not covered

- U4's loop and PSM ripple (the capture-based droop above stands in for them): measure the 3V3 ripple and a burst's dip at bring-up (`.3.9`, `.3.15` compares them with this).
- USB PD voltage transitions: none, the board is 5 V only (ADR 0004).
- A source with foldback or hiccup, and a cable with its own capacitance.
- U5's PSRR (the probe supply's ripple is in `probe-frontend.md`).
